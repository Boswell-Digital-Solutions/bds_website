Extended

Boswell Digital Solutions LLC (BDS) is an independent software engineering and systems design company based in Lexington, Kentucky. Charles Boswell founded the company to build software systems that can be trusted, not merely demonstrated.

Before he wrote software, Boswell worked for ten years as a Wildlife Biology Technician with the United States Forest Service and for nine years as a Forest Ranger Technician III with the Kentucky Division of Forestry. Over sixteen years of wildland fire operations, he led prescribed fire work and qualified as a Fire Incident Commander. Those environments taught a lesson that shapes the company today: systems must work under pressure, processes must be clear, and decisions must be accountable.

BDS operates on a simple principle: humans define intent, systems enforce it, and evidence proves it. Its systems are designed around deterministic workflows, explicit state transitions, enforceable boundaries, transparent behavior, and long-term maintainability. AI is treated as a tool, not an authority, and authority remains with the human operator.

The company's flagship product, AuthorForge, is a local-first authoring system for independent authors and small publishing companies. It is designed to bring drafting, lore, revision, cover preparation, and distributor-ready export into one governed workflow. AuthorForge is in development.

BDS also provides systems engineering services, including architecture, applications, websites, and technical advisory work. The company remains independent and intentionally structured, prioritizing engineering discipline over rapid expansion.

AuthorForge

Status: in development. No public release of AuthorForge has been published yet. Product details below describe its intended design.

In one sentence: AuthorForge is a local-first authoring system that brings drafting, lore, revision, cover preparation, and distributor-ready export into one governed workflow for independent authors and small publishing companies.

The problem

Many authors publish through a stack of separate tools: one for drafting, another for lore, another for covers, and manual fixes after a distributor rejects a file. Each tool solves one part of the work. Publishing is a systems problem, so changes in one part affect the others.

The approach

  • Local-first: manuscripts are stored on the author's own computer.
  • Human-controlled: AI is optional and runs only when the author asks for it.
  • Deterministic export: the same manuscript gives the same output.

“I built AuthorForge because I’m an author. And I was tired of stitching together half-connected tools just to publish a single book.”

— Charles Boswell, founder
